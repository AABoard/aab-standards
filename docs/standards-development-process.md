# Standards Development Process

AAB standards development should be public, traceable, developmentally appropriate, and evidence-aware.

## Stages

1. Topic scoping: define the standard area, audience, and use case.
2. Evidence review: identify registry records, research sources, policy signals, and existing frameworks.
3. Drafting: write clauses, notes, examples, and grade-band progressions.
4. Internal review: check technical accuracy, developmental fit, neutrality, and source trace.
5. Public comment: collect GitHub issues and structured feedback.
6. Response to comments: accept, revise, reject, or defer comments with rationale.
7. Release: tag approved or consultation-ready versions.
8. Maintenance: revise standards as evidence, policy, and practice evolve.

## Required Draft Elements

- Scope and audience.
- Terms and definitions.
- Grade-band or learner-context progression when relevant.
- Normative requirements.
- Non-normative guidance and examples.
- Evidence trace.
- Known limitations.
- Revision history.

## Review Questions

- Is the claim source-traced?
- Is the language appropriate for the grade band or learner context?
- Does the clause avoid endorsing a specific product or provider?
- Does the standard distinguish between current evidence and desired practice?
- Would a school, library, workforce program, or policymaker understand how to apply it?

## Versioning

Use version labels that show maturity:

- `working-draft`
- `public-comment-draft`
- `review-draft`
- `approved-release`
- `maintenance-update`
